<objective>
**As a** solo developer shaping a brand-new epic, **I want to** say what I want to build and then be
argued with about it, **so that** the challenge stage has something real to challenge.

This is the second half of gap **G-05-2**. Plan 05-12 gave the developer a voice mid-conversation.
This plan gives them one at turn zero, and then closes the gap's third missing item: the Product
Kill Criterion comparison, re-staged against the shipped UI.

The evidence is specific. The authoring variant of the launch sheet suppresses the instruction field
(`launch-sheet/LaunchSheet.tsx:760`, the `!isAuthoring &&` guard), and the opening prompt is
`render_epic_prompt(&packet)` (`commands/authoring.rs:198`), assembled wholly from board state. For a
brand-new epic that packet has no epic section, no task roster, no answered assumptions and no
rejected proposals — every section is omitted except the last, which tells the model
(`epic_context_packet.rs:503-509`) that its job is to *challenge the developer's framing*. There is
no framing, and there was no way to supply one.

D-86 is the reason the field was suppressed, and D-86 survives this plan intact: the authoring agent
runs read-only in the project's main checkout unconditionally, so the **permission-mode** control
genuinely does not apply and stays suppressed. The instruction field's suppression was an inference
carried along with it, not a decision, and `05-CONTEXT.md` explicitly leaves "how a session is
started when no epic exists yet, and whether D-27's launch sheet applies" to Claude's discretion.

One door is deliberately left without a premise field: the command-palette `shape-epic` entry
(`board/useBoardQuery.ts::startAuthoringSessionForProject`) starts a fresh session directly. It keeps
working, and the no-framing prompt wording this plan adds is what makes it degrade correctly — the
agent's first move there is to ask, and the composer from 05-12 is how the developer answers. The
sheet is the premeditated door; the palette is the fast one.

Purpose: give the challenge stage something to challenge, and replace a recalled measurement with a
derived one.
Output: a premise that reaches the prompt and the transcript, a prompt that no longer pretends, and
a kill-criterion measurement computed from the board.
</objective>
