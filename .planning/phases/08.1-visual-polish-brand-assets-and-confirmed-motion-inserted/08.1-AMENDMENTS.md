This file records the amendments to LOCKED sections of earlier UI specs that phase 08.1 is permitted
to make. Phase 08.1 is the only phase in the roadmap with that permission (D-02: nothing is fenced).
The price of that permission is D-03: proposal is free, adoption is gated, and every structural change
is its own decision that the owner approves or declines. A structural change is never folded into a
visual task.

Each entry below names the document and the exact section it amends, the date, the decider and the
answer. Plan 09's SUPERSEDES table cites these entries by id. Plans 10, 11 and 12 build only what an
entry marks approved.

The decider for every entry is the owner (Cyril). The agent proposes and records. The agent does not
decide.

## A-01 — The epic roster becomes the home surface

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Decider:** Owner (Cyril)
- **Answer:** approved, as stated
- **Amends:** `.planning/phases/01-the-board-is-the-store-of-record/01-UI-SPEC.md`
  - § Information Architecture & Layout Topology — the "Board list (primary surface, D-01)" subsection
  - § Surface Inventory — the "Board list" row (primary surface)
- **Governing decision:** D-05 (the epic roster leads); D-03 (structural amendments are gated)

**Amendment.** Section Information Architecture & Layout Topology currently makes a flat list, grouped
by the five fixed status bands, the primary surface. The amendment makes the epic roster the home
surface. Each epic row carries:

- title and key
- a progress bar
- a task-distribution strip by status
- a count of agents running on the epic
- a count of proposals waiting on the owner

Expanding an epic reveals its content in place: proposals first, tasks after.

**Consequence the owner accepted.** This is an information-architecture change, not a skin change.
Three things move together: the row model, the virtualizer's size function and the board query's
grouping. The change is costly to unwind, because reverting means rebuilding the flat rack.

**What does NOT change.** One board. One virtualized rack. One keyboard map. One query.

**Rationale.** The owner adopted the Mission Control direction on 2026-10-07. The home surface of the
adopted comps is the epic roster, so A-01 was already implied by that adoption. This entry records it
as its own gated decision, as D-03 requires.

**Grants nothing beyond the above.** A-01 does not amend the Keyboard Map, the Performance Contract or
Row Anatomy. Those are A-02 (grouping key) and A-03 (Row Anatomy and row heights).

**What depends on this.** Plan 05's epic progress aggregate and plan 11's epic row render have a
consumer because A-01 is approved. Had A-01 been declined, both would have lost their consumer.

## A-04 — What an epic row means under the has-a-worktree dimension

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Decider:** Owner (Cyril)
- **Answer:** `any-child`, approved as the recommended reading
- **Amends:** `.planning/phases/01-the-board-is-the-store-of-record/01-UI-SPEC.md`
  - § Information Architecture & Layout Topology > Search and filter (the filter dimension list gains
    a sixth dimension: items that have an active worktree)
- **Boundary:** commits-ahead and dirty are not filter dimensions (git facts, no column, would need a migration)
- **Governing decisions:** D-17 (the sixth dimension, as pure SQL in the core's one filtering query);
  D-03 (no second filtering implementation, and epic semantics is gated rather than guessed);
  RESEARCH A9 (the recommended reading, flagged as the owner's call)

**The question asked.** "Define what an epic row does when the has-a-worktree filter is on." Worktrees
are keyed to tasks, so an epic row can never have one of its own, yet the filter fragment has to say
something about epic rows or the roster loses its headers the moment the chip is used.

**Chosen reading, verbatim:** `any-child`. An epic row matches the "has worktree" dimension when ANY
of its child tasks has an active worktree.

**The three readings offered.**

- `any-child` (recommended): the roster keeps its headers under the chip, and the chip reads as "show
  me the epics with live work". Cost: an epic can appear while most of its tasks are filtered out of it.
- `never`: the chip collapses the view to a flat list of exactly the tasks that have worktrees. Cost:
  turning the chip on destroys the roster's grouping entirely.
- `always`: headers never disappear. Cost: the chip no longer narrows the roster at all, only the
  tasks inside it.

**Facts verified on the real board before the owner answered.**

- `worktrees.task_id` is `NOT NULL UNIQUE REFERENCES tasks(id)`, so an epic can never own a worktree.
  The literal clause is false for every epic row by construction. This is why the question was asked.
- There are 5 active worktrees. All 5 of their tasks belong to epics, in 5 distinct epics, one live
  task each.
- Under `any-child` the roster goes from 13 epics to 5, each showing its one live task, with grouping
  intact. Under `never` it would have collapsed to a flat list of 5 tasks, destroying the home surface
  A-01 established. Under `always` all 13 headers would stay, with 8 rendering as empty shells.

**Implementation consequence.** The epic half of the clause is written against `b.kind = 'epic'`, not
against `epic_id`: plan 05 established that an epic's own `epic_id` is null, so the no-epic bucket
already contains the epic rows. The clause stays inside the one compile-time filter fragment, bound by
a named parameter, and is index-backed by `(project_id, epic_id)` and the unique `worktrees.task_id`.

**Recorded boundary: commits-ahead and dirty are NOT filter dimensions.** This is a deliberate
boundary, not an oversight. They are git facts with no database column. Filtering or sorting by them
would need a materialized column and therefore a migration, which `08.1-CONTEXT.md` lists as deferred.
They remain annotation only (the change cue of A-03).

**What does NOT change.** One board. One filtering query, in the core. No client-side preview, no
renderer-side count estimate, no optimistic narrow.

## A-05 — The change cue's glyphs: `Triangle` and `Dot`, outline-only rule untouched

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Decider:** Owner (Cyril)
- **Answer:** `replace`, with `Triangle` (commits ahead, keeps the count) and `Dot` (uncommitted changes)
- **Amends:** the mark registry's glyph assignments, `packages/desktop/src/legend/marks.ts`
  (`change-ahead`, `change-dirty`). **Not amended:** the registry's glyph rule itself, and
  `01-UI-SPEC.md` § Open Decisions item 3 (the icon-library rule) — neither is touched.
- **Governing decisions:** D-15 (the change cue), D-03 (a structural change is its own gate)

**The question asked.** "Settle the change cue's glyphs, and whether the mark registry's outline-only
rule stays as written."

**The collision.** D-15 writes the cue as a triangle-with-count and a filled dot. The registry's own
rule is that a glyph is a named export from the icon package, outline-only, with no unicode
character standing in for an icon system, and the legend test resolves every registered glyph
against that package. D-15's characters are therefore notation describing an intent, not shippable
glyph strings. Plan 01 registered two real outline glyphs instead: `GitCommitVertical` for commits
ahead and `Circle` for uncommitted changes.

**The three options offered.**

- `keep` — keep the two outline glyphs plan 01 registered. No contract change. Cost: not literally
  the triangle-and-dot D-15 sketched.
- `replace` — use two different outline glyphs, named by the owner. Closer to the intended reading
  while keeping the registry rule intact. Cost: plan 01's registry entries and the legend test
  change with it.
- `amend-rule` — amend the registry rule to permit a filled glyph for data-channel marks. Delivers
  the literal triangle-and-dot. Cost: loosens a published contract for every future mark, because the
  outline-only rule exists so that no mark's meaning rests on fill or colour.

**Chosen option, verbatim:** `replace` — `change-ahead` becomes `Triangle` (the count stays beside
it); `change-dirty` becomes `Dot`.

**Declined: `amend-rule`.** The owner explicitly declined the option that would have permitted filled
glyphs. The outline-only registry rule is UNCHANGED and was NOT loosened. No mark in the registry
may rest its meaning on fill.

**The defect this fixes.** Plan 01 flagged, and left for this plan, that the shipped `Circle` dirty
mark was the same glyph as the Todo status glyph (`strip-todo`) in the same row, told apart only by
position and accessible name. `Dot` is not used by any status mark. (`Dot` is also the glyph of
`project-current`, which lives in the left rail, a different surface and channel; it never shares a
row with the change cue.)

**Verified before the answer was given.** `Triangle`, `Dot`, `PencilLine`, `Circle` and
`GitCommitVertical` all exist as named exports in the installed `lucide-react`; the legend test's
resolve-every-glyph assertion passes with the new names.

**Applied in the same task (not left as follow-up).**

- `packages/desktop/src/legend/marks.ts` — the two registry entries.
- `packages/desktop/src/board/BoardRow.tsx` — the two fallback names beside the registry lookups
  (`changeAheadMark?.glyph ?? "Triangle"`, `changeDirtyMark?.glyph ?? "Dot"`).
- `packages/desktop/src/legend/__tests__/legend.test.tsx` — the agent-channel expectations. The
  per-channel count (17) is unchanged because the entries were renamed, not added.

**Grants nothing beyond the above.** A-05 does not grant a filled glyph for any mark, does not
change the change cue's slot or placement (A-03), and does not make commits-ahead or dirty filter
dimensions (A-04).

## A-06 — Selection: the 2px neutral rule against the craft floor, and the focus clause

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Decider:** Owner (Cyril)
- **Answer:** `2px rule` — `border-left: 2px solid var(--text-primary-rack)` plus the row-surface shift, exactly D-12, approved
- **Craft floor:** `craft-floor.md` line 35 (a coloured side border above 1px on a list item) is read as targeting accent colours, not neutral ink. The detector did not and cannot rule on it; see "What the detector said".
- **Amends:** `.planning/phases/01-the-board-is-the-store-of-record/01-UI-SPEC.md`
  - § Color and Token Roles — the row-state roles (`--row-surface-selected`, `--row-surface-selected-unfocused`)
  - § Accessibility — the obligation that a selected-but-unfocused row look visibly different from a focused one (clause A-06(b) below)
- **Governing decisions:** D-12 (selection is a rule plus a background shift, never a brand colour); D-03
- **Follow-up:** two new tokens and a plan 14 build, specified in A-06(b)

### A-06(a) — The 2px rule

**The question asked.** "Choose the row selection treatment, given what the mechanical craft detector
actually said about the comped row."

**The four options offered.**

- `rule-2px` — the 2px neutral rule as D-12 specifies. Strongest read of "you are here". Cost: may trip
  the craft floor's side-border clause, which would make it a standing exception.
- `rule-1px` — a one-pixel rule plus the background shift. Inside the craft floor with no exception.
  Cost: a weaker cursor in a hairline-heavy world where 1px rules are already everywhere.
- `inset-bar` — an inset bar drawn as a pseudo-element rather than a border, so the clause does not
  apply. Keeps the 2px weight. Cost: one more drawing mechanism on the hottest row in the product.
- `background-only` — background shift only, no rule. Quietest. Cost: all four row states must be kept
  distinguishable with background alone, which is the hardest version of that obligation.

**Chosen option, verbatim:** `2px rule`. `border-left: 2px solid var(--text-primary-rack)` plus the
row-surface shift, exactly as D-12 states.

**What the detector said (real output, plan 04).** `detect.mjs` ran in full parse mode against the
three comps and `comp.css` after the owner authorised installing its four parsers. Result: **135
findings, all severity `warning`, zero errors** — 115 `cramped-padding`, 14 `tiny-text`, 5
`undersized-ui-text`, 1 `flat-type-hierarchy`. Full output: `comps/detector-report.json`.

**The detector's silence on selection is NOT approval.** It emitted nothing about selection because
the side-border ban is not a `detect.mjs` antipattern. `skill-ban-side-stripe-borders` is prose in
`craft-floor.md` ("a *colored* `border-left` or `border-right` above 1px on cards, list items,
callouts, or alerts"). The detector has no rule for it, so it could not flag the 2px rule and it could
not clear it. Silence here is structural, not a verdict. **This entry must not be read as "the craft
detector cleared the 2px rule". It did not and it could not.** The decision below rests on judgement,
not on a measurement.

**Rationale (the owner's reading, recorded as a deliberate judgement).** The ban's operative word is
"colored". The side-stripe pattern it targets is an accent-coloured stripe used as decoration on
cards, callouts and alerts, repeated down a page. The selection rule here is the opposite: a single
neutral-ink mark, exactly one per list, that marks the current position and carries no brand or data
colour. Brand colour stays on progress (D-12). The owner accepts the reading that the ban targets
accent colours rather than neutral ink.

**Standing exception, stated plainly.** If the ban is later read to cover neutral ink as well, this
rule stands as a **knowing exception recorded here**, not as an oversight. A later reviewer who finds
`border-left: 2px` on a list item has found a decided thing with a dated reason, not a miss.

**How the four row states stay simultaneously distinguishable.** Idle: no rule, transparent surface.
Hover: no rule, `--row-surface-hover`. Selected and focused: the full-ink 2px rule, `--row-surface-selected`
and the 2px focus outline. Selected and unfocused: the lighter 2px rule of A-06(b) and
`--row-surface-selected-unfocused`. The rule is the channel that separates "selected" from the other
two; the surface values alone cannot (see A-06(b) for the measured reason).

### A-06(b) — The focus clause: a selected row without list focus must look different

**Why it is in this entry.** While preparing A-06 the executor measured the four row surfaces and found
that `selected` and `selected-unfocused` are effectively the same colour. The orchestrator recomputed
the ratios independently from `tokens.css` and confirmed them; the executor reproduced them a third
time:

| Pair | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| idle vs selected | 1.065 | 1.033 |
| **selected vs selected-unfocused** | **1.011** | **1.014** |
| hover vs selected | 1.028 | 1.021 |

A 1.01 ratio is below any threshold at which two flat surfaces are told apart. One accuracy note: today
`BoardRow.tsx` also paints a 2px `--focus-ring` outline on the focused row (line 302), which is the only
thing that makes the two states differ in the shipped build. That outline is a keyboard-focus indicator,
not a selection cue. Leaving it as the only focused/unfocused difference would mean the selection
treatment itself, the new rule, carries no focus information of its own.

**The decision.** The owner chose to fix this in this phase rather than queue it: accessibility is the
one thing this phase does not defer and the token layer is already open. **The rule is full ink when the
list has focus, and lighter when it has lost focus.**

**Binding values** (computed in this task; the text and surface roles they sit on are unchanged):

| Role | Light | Dark | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `--selection-rule` | `#1f2328` | `#ededed` | Selected and the list has focus. Equal to `--text-primary-rack`. |
| `--selection-rule-unfocused` | `#7a828c` | `#707070` | Selected, list has lost focus. Equal in value to `--border-control`. |

Measured:

| Measure | Light | Dark |
|---|---|---|
| focused rule vs unfocused rule | **4.06** | **4.23** |
| focused rule vs idle surface | 15.80 | 16.91 |
| unfocused rule vs idle surface | 3.89 | 4.00 |
| unfocused rule vs hover / selected / unfocused surfaces (lowest) | 3.65 | 3.87 |

The unfocused rule clears 3:1 against every row surface in both modes (non-text contrast), so it stays
visible as a rule. The focused-versus-unfocused separation is 4.06 and 4.23, against the 1.011 and 1.014
of the surfaces. Both states keep a **2px** width, with a 2px transparent left border reserved on every
task row, so a row changing state never reflows its content. The difference is tone, not width.

**Why two new tokens and not a re-use.** `--border-control` already holds the unfocused value, but its
meaning is "a control's visible boundary". Aliasing a selection state to it would let a later change to
control borders silently change selection. The two roles are separate names that currently share a value.

**What this does NOT grant.** It does not change the surface values (`--row-surface-selected` and
`--row-surface-selected-unfocused` stay as shipped), the focus-ring outline, or the Keyboard Map. It
does not add a third selection state.

**The legend test does not cover this class of defect.** `legend.test.tsx` (the
"row-state surfaces ... are four pairwise-distinct values" case, lines 15-21) asserts only that the four
hex strings differ. `#f6f8fa` and `#f8f9fb` differ as strings and pass, at a 1.011 ratio no eye can
separate. That test proves the values are not identical; it does not prove they are perceptible. A later
reader must not take it for coverage of the "selected-but-unfocused must look different" obligation. The
test plan 14 adds (below) is the first one that does.

### A-06 addendum 1 — the selected band stops at the AA ceiling (08.1-14 finish review)

- **Date:** 2026-10-08
- **Decider:** the independent finish reviewer proposed it; the executor applied it as far as AA allows and recorded the ceiling. Not an owner decision, and not a loosening of any floor.
- **What the reviewer asked.** Raise `--row-surface-selected` to at least 1.12 (light) and 1.08 (dark) against the ground, and give `--row-surface-selected-unfocused` the idle surface, so the focused and unfocused states differ by band-present against band-absent rather than by a 2px tick changing from ink to grey.
- **What was measured.** Every text role sits on the selected surface, so the band is capped by the weakest one. Mixing the ground toward ink: light runs out at about 1.08 (`--blocked-text-rack`, 4.87 on the ground, falls to 4.50), dark at about 1.04 (the dark brand text lift `#5175e1`, 4.70 on the ground, falls to 4.50). At the reviewer's targets the worst text role is 4.3 (light) and 4.3 (dark). **The reviewer's numbers are not reachable.** `contrast.test.ts` now asserts that, so the claim is runnable.
- **What was applied.** Light selected `#f6f8fa` to `#f5f7f9` (1.065 to 1.074; worst text role 4.533). Dark selected `#0f0f0f` to `#101010` (1.033 to 1.040; worst 4.514). Both are the largest step that keeps every text role at 4.5 with the cool tint, and the gain is small on purpose: the surfaces were already at the ceiling, which is why plan 07 found them weak.
- **What the reviewer could not have known.** Reaching their target needs a text role darkened or lifted, not a band change: the blocked text darker in light, the brand text lift raised well past its "minimum step that clears AA" in dark. Either is a data-channel or brand value, which this phase pins. If the owner ever wants a visibly stronger band, that is the decision to make, and it is a trade against those two colours.
- **The unfocused state.** A task row now sits on the IDLE surface when selected without list focus, and keeps the lighter 2px rule (3.65 to 4.0 against the ground). So focus reads twice: surface present and ink rule, against surface absent and grey rule. The `--row-surface-selected-unfocused` token stays, at `#f9fafc` / `#0e0e0e`, for the epic header, which has no rule: giving it the idle surface would leave a selected epic row with no mark at all once the list loses focus.
- **Amends.** The two surface values and the unfocused row of the A-06(b) table; nothing else. The rule roles, the focus-ring offset, the Keyboard Map and the "no third selection state" clause are untouched.

## A-08 — The decline path, the orphan modal's third answer, and two brand rulings

### A-08(a) — The review surface gets a decline path, in this phase

**The finding.** "Request changes" is drawn in the approved comp but has **never existed in the
source** — `git log -S "Request changes"` returns nothing. So on the surface where this product's
core value becomes a daily act, an agent's work can be merged but never sent back. Plan 08.1-16 was
a finish pass and correctly did not invent a feature; this is a Phase 8 scope gap that finishing
exposed.

**Decided:** add it in this phase, as its own plan (08.1-21) with its own tests, **before** plan
08.1-20 closes. Plan 20 writes `DESIGN.md` from the built world, so the decline path must exist
first or the document will describe a review surface that cannot say no.

**Shape the owner was shown:** a Request-changes action returning the task to the agent with the
unchecked criteria as the reason.

### A-08(b) — `OrphanRecoveryModal` gains "Leave them running"

**The finding.** The modal offers three `Kill` buttons and `Kill All`, with no Escape binding, no
click-outside dismissal and no close button — deliberate, per `D-46`/`T-03-10-01` — and it closes
only when its own row list empties. A developer who wants to KEEP three live agent sessions
therefore has no way out of the application.

**Decided:** add a non-destructive `Leave them running` on the hairline, left of `Kill All`.

**This does NOT weaken D-46.** That contract says the overlay cannot be dismissed without answering.
Adding a second kind of answer leaves "you must answer" intact; it only stops "kill" being the sole
answer available. Recorded here because it changes Phase 3 behaviour and must not look like drift.

Separately, and not part of this decision: initial focus moved off the destructive `Kill` button.
`board/DeleteConfirm.tsx` already focused `Cancel` for exactly that reason, with a comment saying so.
