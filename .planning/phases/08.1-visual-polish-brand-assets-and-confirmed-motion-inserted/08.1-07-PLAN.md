<assumption_delta_decision>
**Detector fired** (`assumption-delta scan 8.1`: `detected: true`; signals `pluralization`,
`chosen`). The real transition in this phase's scope is in the token layer, and it is a genuine
singular-to-plural: the product today has **one** material world, hardcoded as a cream strip on a
dark rack. D-10 introduces a **second** mode, and the existing spec "structurally cannot" express
it.

- **Noun that is now primary:** *theme mode*. A colour role is no longer a value; it is a value
  **per mode**.
- **Decision: promote.** The typed theme constants become `LIGHT` and `DARK` mirrors of one role
  set, and the three existing mode-blind constant groups are retired rather than kept alongside a
  new dark group. Adding dark *alongside* the still-required mode-blind objects would let a future
  role be declared in one mode only and still typecheck — the exact failure RESEARCH Pitfall 3
  describes at the CSS level, reproduced at the TypeScript level.
- **Companion invariant test (accepted):** `contrast.test.ts` iterates the role set and asserts
  every role is present and clears its floor **for every supported mode**, driven by a mode list
  rather than by two hand-written blocks. That test goes red the instant a future phase adds a role
  in one mode only, so the singular assumption cannot be reintroduced silently.
- **Second, smaller instance:** the one shared row-height constant becomes a lookup by row kind.
  Same decision — promote, not add-alongside — and plan 10 carries it.
</assumption_delta_decision>

<objective>
Land the adopted direction in the only two files permitted to hold a colour literal, and re-derive
every contrast number against the new grounds.

Implements **D-10** (one token set authored for light and dark; near-neutral ground, one surface
family, 1px hairline rules, no fills), **D-11** (colour only as a data channel — the five paper
tints go), **D-12** (the progress fill carries the brand hue; selection is a rule plus a background
shift, not a brand colour) and **D-19** (the measured palette is re-derived, not recolored; the pin
is on the hue, not the hex; the table is not the proof, the runnable assertion is).

**What D-19 voids, precisely.** `01-UI-SPEC.md` § "Pinned brand, and the lifts it requires" measured
`#7d99ff` at 5.92:1 on the rack and `#2d4fb8` at 4.97:1 on the strip. Those figures were correct for
the cream-strip-on-dark-rack pairing and are void now, because both grounds change.
`08.1-RESEARCH.md` Pattern 2 re-measured with this project's own formula and found something the old
two-lift model gets backwards: the brand `#3c64dd` **already clears AA for text on the new light
ground** (5.17:1 on white, 4.86:1 on the light surface), so light needs no lift at all, while on the
new dark ground it reads 3.83:1 and 3.71:1 — fine for a non-text progress fill, failing for text, so
**dark still needs one**. Applying "both lifts" would put `#7d99ff` on white at 2.67:1, less
accessible than the brand itself.

**Re-theme by value, not by name.** Every existing custom-property name stays. Counted over `src/`,
the divider and primary-text names are each used in 41 files and the chrome radius in 36; renaming
them is a fifty-three-file diff with no visible effect, and the names become semantic debt to clean
up after the owner is actually using the app. Changing values is two files.

Probe fallback: skipped — this phase has no REQ-IDs to probe (visible skip). `must_haves` derive
from SC0–SC4, the CONTEXT decisions and `08.1-VALIDATION.md`'s requirement-to-test map.
