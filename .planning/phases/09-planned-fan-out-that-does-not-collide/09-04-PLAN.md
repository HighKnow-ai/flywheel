<objective>
**Phase goal (ROADMAP `**Goal**`, verbatim — it is not in `As a / I want to / so that` form):** Cyril turns
one epic into several agents working at once, with parallelism bounded by real file-set disjointness rather
than optimism.

One global number: how many agent sessions may run at once across every project. Stored, validated,
readable, and shown next to what is actually running.

Purpose: PLAN-06 and ROADMAP success criterion 4. A per-project cap cannot bound what actually runs out —
five projects at three each is fifteen sessions on one account's rate and session limits, on one machine.
Research anchors the ceiling: a public measurement had 8 of 13 agents fail at 13-way parallelism with no
spawn staggering. This project already staggers spawns and retries commit-time lock contention, so git
contention is the least binding constraint; the account's limits and the user's own review bandwidth are
the binding ones.

Output: `dispatch_max_parallel` with a default of 3 and a validated range of 1 to 8, the occupancy read,
the command surface, and the Settings control.
</objective>

## Recorded decisions (left to the planner by CONTEXT.md, not re-asked)

- **Cap default 3, validated range 1 to 8.** The measured failure point in the public report is 13-way
  parallelism with no staggering. This project enforces a 400 ms per-repository spawn floor and retries
  commit-time lock contention up to five attempts, so three agents ramp up in 0.8 s of floor time and
  leave more than five slots of margin under the measured failure point. The ceiling of 8 is a judgement,
  not a measurement, and is one constant for the owner to change.
- **Authoring and planning runs count for occupancy, but are never refused.** They are agent sessions on
  the same account, so they hold a slot. The cap gates the dispatcher's own spawns only — which is exactly
  what success criterion 4 says ("the planner never exceeds it"), and never what a user-initiated launch is
  allowed to do.
