<objective>
WORK-07's first two thirds and the first half of ROADMAP criterion 5: **the user sees every worktree across
all projects with its disk usage.** Plan 10-09 adds the reclaim batch.

Purpose: disk accumulates across a dozen worktrees in four large repositories and nothing in the product
shows it today. D-178's whole move is to derive reclaimability from facts already recorded rather than to
invent a populator for a column nobody writes — research confirmed independently that
`worktrees.status`'s CHECK constraint allows `stale` and `merged` but **no code in the repository ever
writes either**; the only writers are the reconcile pass and the recreate path, both writing `active` or
`missing`. WORK-07's word "stale" therefore has no existing meaning, and this plan declines to mint one.

Two measured constraints shape the implementation. A recursive walk over a repository carrying
`node_modules` and a Rust `target/` costs seconds, so it must never run under the database mutex or on the
IPC thread, and it must never walk a filesystem nobody is looking at (D-177). And the inventory covers
**task** worktrees only: `worktrees.task_id` is unique and not-null, so epic integration worktrees are real
directories under the same root with no row at all (Finding 8) — a limit the empty-state copy must state
rather than hide.

Output: `reclaim_eligibility` and `worktree_size` in the core, the two-phase `worktree_inventory_inputs` /
`finish_worktree_inventory` read, the `worktree_inventory` and `worktree_measure` commands, the inventory
view with its chips, and the project rail's worktrees entry point.
</objective>
